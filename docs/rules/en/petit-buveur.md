# Rules of The Little Drinker

The party board game: a die, pawns, squares with effects and dares — playable **free online with 2 to 99 players** (a player who leaves is replaced by a bot) or locally on a single phone.

## The idea

Everyone moves along the board to the roll of the die. Every square has its own personality: dares, sips handed out or taken, table-wide events, special squares that shake up the race.

## How a game plays out

1. **On your turn**, roll the die — your pawn moves square by square.
2. **The square is revealed**: apply its effect (dare, swap, event…).
3. Some squares trigger mini-moments: Sip Wheel, heads or tails, Dice of shame…
4. The first to reach the end of the board wins the game.

## The square families

The board mixes several families you can recognise by their colours: the classics (drink/hand out), dares to complete, group events, and special squares that target a specific player.

## Victory

Reach the last square first. Online, the win counts towards the site's leaderboard.

## Tips

- Refused dares cost more than attempted ones.
- Keep an eye on your opponents' pawns: some squares let you target them.

## Drinking mode

This is the drinking game par excellence — each "sip" remains a game unit: water, soda or whatever you like. Locally, the Soft mode turns sips and alcohol-related dares into forfeits and points; online, The Little Drinker isn't part of the alcohol-free catalogue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play without a game master?

Yes. The die, the squares and their effects are handled by the game; dares appear on screen and the table judges whether they were pulled off. Nobody has to referee.

### Can you play remotely?

Yes: an online table, a code or a QR to share, and voice chat for the dares. And locally, a single phone passed around the table is all you need.

### Can you play without alcohol?

Locally, yes: the Soft mode replaces sips with forfeits and points, and the alcohol-themed dares with other ones. Online, The Little Drinker stays a sip game — sips are game units, everyone puts whatever they like in their glass.

### Can you play alone, with bots?

Yes: in the lobby, the host fills the table with bots, or starts alone with them. If someone leaves mid-game, a bot takes over their pawn after 3 minutes — the game never gets stuck.

### How long does a game last?

A 30-square board and a 6-sided die: about ten turns per player, plus the squares that send you back. With 4 players, count on 20 to 30 minutes.

### Does it work with a big crowd?

Up to 99 online, in theory. In practice, beyond 8 players you wait a long time for your turn: split into two tables.
