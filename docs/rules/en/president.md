# Rules of Président online — combos, the 2 cuts and the Trou

**Président** (also known as "Trou du c…" — think President/Asshole — depending on the schoolyard) on Le Pillaveur: 54 cards (52 + 2 jokers), ever-stronger combos, and a ruthless pecking order. Free, 4 to 8 players, each on their own phone — hands stay truly secret.

## How it works

1. All 54 cards (2 jokers included) are dealt. Card order is Président's: **3 lowest… Ace, then 2 highest**.
2. The first player lays down a **combo**: a single card, a pair, three of a kind or four of a kind (same rank).
3. The next players must lay down **the same number of cards**, of a **higher — or equal** rank (equal is allowed here, and it locks the trick: see "Queen or nothing" below) — or pass.
4. When everyone passes, the last player to lay down **wins the trick** and leads whatever they want.

## House variants (active online)

- **Equal is allowed**: you can lay down a card (or a combo) of the SAME value as the one on top. But watch out…
- **"Queen or nothing"**: after an equal play, the trick's rank is locked — the next player matches it in turn or **their turn is skipped**. As long as people keep matching, the rule continues.
- **Closing the four**: if 3 cards of the same value come out in a row, whoever holds the 4th can slam it down **even out of turn**: the trick is closed and they lead again. Same with pairs — a pair of 5s is played, the other pair of 5s closes it. However, **three of a kind laid down in one go** can't be closed with a single card: you only close with a play the same size as the ones in the trick.
- **Nobody plays after the President**: as soon as the first player out lays down their last card, the trick is burned — the next player leads whatever they want.
- **2 chameleon jokers**: a joker replaces any card. It completes a combo (a 7 + a joker = a pair of 7s), and played alone it copies the card on top — which triggers the "or nothing" rule. On a **free trick**, though, a joker played alone opens at the lowest rank: it doesn't take anyone's value, it just opens. It can also be used to close a four.
- **Positions survive the rematch**: on "Play again", the President, the Trou and the vices from the previous game keep their roles (card exchanges from the very first round). If one of them has left the table, the closest player in the ranking takes over the position.
- **Trick history**: during the round, a panel folds/unfolds the list of previous tricks — who played what, and who won each trick.

## The 2 cuts

The 2 is the supreme card: it **wins the trick immediately**. Whoever cuts leads again right away — a formidable weapon, not to be wasted.

## President, Trou… and their vices

- The first to empty their hand is **President** 👑, the second **Vice-President** 🎖️.
- The last one holding cards is the **Trou** 🕳️, the second-to-last **Vice-Trou** 🪠.
- Between rounds, the exchanges are **automatic**: the Trou gives their **2 best cards** to the President (who gives back their 2 worst), and the Vice-Trou gives their **best card** to the Vice-President (who gives back their worst). No haggling, no cheating — the server handles it.
- The Trou leads the next round (cold comfort).

## How a game plays out

- The host picks the number of rounds (**1, 3 or 5**).
- **30 seconds per turn** — a sleepy player **passes automatically**. If they were supposed to open the trick (where passing isn't allowed), the server plays their **lowest card** for them.
- Between two rounds, a 15-second **end-of-round screen** shows the ranking and the cards exchanged. It moves on by itself when time's up, and nobody can skip it before the table has had time to read it.
- The final ranking is that of the **last round**: its President wins the game (wins and ranks count toward the online leaderboard).
- In With alcohol mode, the Trou drinks. In Soft mode, they only lose their pride.

## Good to know

- **4 players minimum**, up to 8 — bots fill the table. They each have their own temperament: the aggressive one overbids and cuts readily, the cautious one lets tricks that are too high go, the joker sometimes opens sideways. All of them keep their 2s for cutting, avoid breaking up three of a kind for nothing, and know how to **close a four out of turn**.
- Anti-cheat: opponents' hands never leave the server, only card counts are shared.
- TV mode shows the table in the middle and card counts around it.

## Variants and player counts

All 54 cards are always dealt: what changes is hand size, and with it the pace.

- **4 players**: 13 or 14 cards each, and everyone has a position — President, Vice-President, Vice-Trou, Trou. Rounds are long, and the exchanges between rounds weigh heavily (two cards each way).
- **5 to 6 players**: 9 to 11 cards. One or two players in the middle escape the exchanges; it's the most balanced format.
- **7 to 8 players**: only 6 to 8 cards. Lightning rounds, where a well-kept 2 or joker decides the crown.
- **Fewer than 4**: impossible — the President/Trou and Vice/Vice exchanges need four positions. The host fills in with bots.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play without a game master?

Yes. Dealing, combo validity, the 2 cut, closing the four, exchanges between rounds: the server does it all, and nobody can cheat with their hand.

### Can you play remotely?

Yes, and only online: hands stay secret on each phone, only card counts are shared. Voice chat for roasting the Trou.

### Can you play alcohol-free?

Yes. Président is part of the site's Alcohol-free mode: the Trou only loses their pride.

### Can you play solo, with bots?

Yes. "Try it with bots" launches a private table with three bots of different temperaments; your friends can join mid-game.

### How long does a game last?

30 seconds per turn at most, 15 seconds between rounds. A round lasts 5 to 8 minutes: just one for a quick pre-drinks game, five for a full night (30 to 40 minutes).

### Do you need an account?

No: a nickname is enough, the guest account is created on the spot, no e-mail. You can save it later to keep your stats.
