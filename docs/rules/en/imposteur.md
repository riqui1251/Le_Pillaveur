# Rules of The Impostor online — close words, clues and a vote

Two almost identical words, an intruder who doesn't know it, and plenty of bad
faith: **The Impostor** is played **free online with 3 to 16 players**,
each on their own phone, with optional voice chat.

## The idea

The game draws a **pair of close words** — *beach* and *pool*, *coffee* and
*tea*. Most of the table gets one of them; the **impostor** (or impostors)
gets the other.

The game's big cruelty: **nobody knows which side they're on**. You see your
word, and that's it. That "sun" everyone is describing might be yours… or
not.

## How a round plays out

1. **The word**: everyone discovers their word on their screen during the
   starting countdown. Nobody else sees it.
2. **The clues**: one at a time, each player writes **one short clue**
   (30 characters max, 45 seconds per player). You can't write your own word,
   or a piece of it that gives too much away — the server rejects the clue. A
   silent or too-slow player posts a "…".
3. **The vote**: once everyone has gone, each player votes **in secret** for
   the most suspicious player (60 seconds; you can't vote for yourself,
   latecomers abstain).
4. **The reveal**: the vote count is public, and the eliminated player leaves
   with **word and side exposed**. A tie, or no votes? Nobody leaves — and
   it's another round of clues among the survivors.

## Variants and player counts

The number of impostors follows the size of the table: just one by default,
**two from 7 players**. The host can adjust it in the lobby, as long as that
side is **strictly in the minority** at the start (and three impostors at
most).

| Players | Impostors by default | Adjustable up to |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to 4 | 1 | 1 |
| 5 to 6 | 1 | 2 |
| 7 to 16 | 2 | 3 |

- With **3 players**, the impostors win as soon as only 2 survivors remain
  (3 on any other table): a single wrong vote and it's over.
- Between **5 and 8**, one round is often enough to unmask the intruder — the
  most nerve-racking format.
- With **10 or more**, each clue gets lost in the crowd: switch to two
  or three impostors, otherwise the village wins too easily.

## Victory

- **The village wins** as soon as **all** the impostors have been eliminated.
- **The impostors win** if they make it to the **last 3 survivors**
  (the last 2 on a 3-player table), or as soon as they're **no longer in the
  minority** among the living.

There's no "last chance": an unmasked impostor has lost, even if they
had guessed the village's word.

## Tips

- Give "sideways" clues: linked to your word, never a definition —
  a clue that's too precise unmasks you if you're the intruder, and tips off
  the intruder if you're not.
- You don't know if you're the impostor: the others' doubt is yours
  too. A clue that's too vague makes you look guilty.
- Watch who bounces off the previous clues rather than their own word.
- Eliminated players stay on as **neutral** spectators: their screen gives
  away no secrets, so they can stay on voice chat without spoiling the game.

## Drinking mode

- An **innocent wrongly eliminated** drinks **3 sips**.
- An **unmasked impostor** drinks **2 sips per survivor** — the earlier they
  fall, the lighter the bill.
- At the end: if the village wins, the impostor finishes their drink; if
  they slip through the net, the civilians drink up.

In soft mode, the counters disappear completely (sips are game units,
everyone puts whatever they like in their glass).

## Good to know

- **Anti-cheat**: words and sides never leave the server.
  Nobody, not even TV mode, sees a living player's side.
- A player who leaves the table is replaced by a bot after a grace
  period — they can come back before then.
- The reveal moves on by itself: the game never hangs on a player who
  dozed off, and nobody can skip the others' screen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play without a game master?

Yes. The words are dealt, the clues timestamped, the votes counted —
everything is automatic, and the reveal moves on without anyone having to
click. The server is the only one that knows the sides.

### Can you play remotely?

Yes, and only online: everyone gets their word in secret on their
phone. A code to share, voice chat optional — clues are written,
the debate is spoken.

### Can you play without alcohol?

Yes. The Impostor is part of the site's alcohol-free mode: the sip counters
disappear, the rules don't change.

### Can you play alone, with bots?

Yes. "Try it with bots" opens a private table with two bots; your
friends can join along the way. The bots vote at random and are
deliberately weak: perfect for learning, not for detective work.

### How long does a game last?

45 seconds per clue, 60 seconds to vote: a round with 6 players lasts 5 to
6 minutes, and it often takes two or three rounds. Count on 10 to 20 minutes.

### Do you need an account?

No: a nickname is enough, the guest account is created on the spot, no email
needed. You can save it later to keep your stats.
