# Rules of Sans Filtre — the fill-in-the-blank card game

**Sans Filtre** ("No Filter") is the online party game where bad faith is a virtue: a black card to complete, anonymous answers laid down by the whole table, and a judge who crowns the funniest. 4 to 16 players, free, with built-in voice chat — it's made to be read out loud.

## How it works

Each round, a **black card with a blank** shows up for the whole table, for example: "Tonight, everything went off the rails because of ___." Every player (except the round judge) picks from their **hand of 7 answer cards** the one that completes the sentence in the funniest — or most awkward — way.

Answers are **anonymous**: nobody knows who played what until the crowning.

## The judge

The **judge** role rotates every round. The judge doesn't play a card: they discover the answers laid down, **read them out loud** on voice chat (that's where the table cracks up), then **crown** their favorite.

The author of the crowned card wins **a crown** and their identity is revealed to the table. Playing Sans Filtre well is less about being funny in the abstract than about aiming for **the judge's sense of humor**.

## How a game plays out

1. The host picks the number of rounds (5, 8 or 12) and opens the table.
2. Each round: black card → everyone plays an answer (60 seconds) → the judge reads and crowns → the author is revealed, +1 crown.
3. After each round, players draw a new card: the hand stays at 7.
4. At the end of the rounds, **the player with the most crowns wins the game**.

## Good to know

- **4 players minimum** (one judge and at least three answers to choose from), up to 16 — it's a big-table game.
- The card content is **in French** and follows a charter: cheeky by innuendo yes, degrading no.
- In **Soft mode**, only the tame cards are dealt (nothing about alcohol) — perfect for playing with family.
- A player who leaves the game is replaced by a bot after 3 minutes; bots play cards and, as soon as the table has two humans, are never judges.
- TV mode shows the black card big, and the answers while the judge reads — ideal for a party.

## Variants and player counts

The hand stays at 7 cards whatever the player count; what changes is the number of answers to read… and how long the judge spends on them.

- **4 players**: one judge, three answers per round. You quickly spot everyone's style — anonymity barely holds, that's the charm of a small group.
- **6 to 10 players**: cruising speed. Enough answers for anonymity to hold, few enough for the reading to stay lively.
- **11 to 16 players**: up to 15 answers to read per round. Pick 5 rounds, and a judge with stamina; TV mode helps the whole table follow the reading.
- **With bots**: they play cards but, as soon as there are two humans at the table, are **never judges** — the role only rotates among humans. Alone against bots, they join the rotation: otherwise you'd judge every round without ever playing a card.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play without a game master?

Yes. The judge rotates automatically every round; the game deals the hands, shuffles the answers and reveals the crowned author. The only human job is reading out loud.

### Can you play remotely?

Yes, and only online: everyone keeps their hand secret on their phone, impossible with a single device. The judge reads the answers on voice chat.

### Can you play alcohol-free?

Yes. In Alcohol-free mode, only the tame cards are dealt — nothing about alcohol, same crowns.

### Can you play solo, with bots?

Possible, but not much fun: alone against three bots, the judge role rotates between them and you, and a bot crowns at random. Sans Filtre is a big-table game; bots are there to fill in, not to replace.

### How long does a game last?

60 seconds to play a card, up to 90 for the judge to read and crown: about two minutes per round. With 5, 8 or 12 rounds, count on 10 to 25 minutes.

### Do you need an account?

No: a nickname is enough, the guest account is created on the spot, no e-mail. You can save it later to keep your crowns.
