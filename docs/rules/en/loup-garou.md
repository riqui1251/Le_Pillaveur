# Rules of Werewolf online

The ultimate party classic, playable **free online with 4 to 12 players**: everyone gets a secret role on their own phone, the wolves strike at night, the village debates and votes by day — with **built-in voice chat**, a written village chat and TV mode to put the game up on the big screen.

## The table

- **4 to 12 players** — empty seats can be filled with bots.
- **Wolves**: 1 below 6 players, 2 from 6 to 10, 3 at 11-12 (an "Extra wolf" option at 5 players).
- **The Seer is always in play.** The other special roles — Witch, Hunter, Bodyguard, Raven, Elder — rotate from one game to the next to keep things fresh.

## Variants and player counts

The line-up is drawn every game based on the number of players: the wolf count is set by the table size, the Seer is always there, and the other special roles are drawn at random within a limit that guarantees some "plain" villagers.

| Players | Wolves | Special roles drawn (plus the Seer) | Plain villagers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1 | 0 or 1 | 1 to 2 |
| 5 | 1 (2 with the Extra wolf option) | 0 or 1 | 2 to 3 (1 to 2 with the extra wolf) |
| 6 to 8 | 2 | up to 3 | 0 to 5 (at 6, a table with no plain villager is possible) |
| 9 to 10 | 2 | up to 4 | 2 to 7 |
| 11 to 12 | 3 | up to 4 | 3 to 8 |

- **With 4**, a single wolf against three: one night, one debate, one vote — the game plays out in one or two days. The Extra wolf option is refused at 4 (two wolves would win straight away).
- **With 5**, the "Extra wolf" option makes the pack fearsome: save it for tables that know the game.
- **From 6 to 8**, it's the classic format: two wolves, the Witch or the Hunter often in play, three to four days of play. Each special role shows up roughly every other game: at 6, the whole village sometimes has a power.
- **From 9 to 12**, plan for a longer debate (the host sets it from 1 to 5 minutes): with three wolves and up to four special roles, the accusations fly.

## The roles

- **Werewolf** 🐺 — every night, votes for a victim with their accomplices. The wolves know each other and share a private chat.
- **Seer** — probes one player per night and learns their side.
- **Witch** — two potions for the whole game: the life potion saves the wolves' victim, the death potion takes out a player.
- **Hunter** — on dying, fires one last bullet and takes a player of their choice down with them.
- **Bodyguard** — protects one player from the wolves each night, never the same one two nights in a row.
- **Raven** — secretly marks a player: the public mark puts **+2 votes against them** in the day vote.
- **Elder** — survives the wolves' first attack (but not the vote, nor the potion).
- **Villager** — no power: just observation, debate and a vote.

## How a game plays out

1. **Reveal**: everyone discovers their secret role on their screen.
2. **Night**: the Bodyguard protects, the Seer probes, the Raven marks, the wolves pick their victim, the Witch brews. As soon as a role has acted, the phase tightens to keep the pace up.
3. **Dawn**: the night's dead are announced, roles revealed.
4. **Mayoral election** (first day): the Mayor's vote counts double at the lynching.
5. **Debate**: on voice chat or in the village chat. The bots accuse and defend themselves too!
6. **Vote**: everyone names a suspect. A tie? Revote between the tied players; another tie, and nobody is lynched.
7. And night falls again…

## Victory

- **The village wins** when no wolf is left alive.
- **The wolves win** when they are as many as the other survivors.

## Drinking mode (optional)

When it's drinks time, "sips" set the rhythm: 3 when you die, 2 for the whole village when an innocent is lynched, 3 per wolf when an accomplice is unmasked. In **soft mode**, the sips disappear — the game stays exactly the same.

## Tips

- Seer: don't reveal your info too early, you become target no. 1.
- Wolves: accuse in moderation — overacting gets spotted.
- Village: watch who votes like whom; packs rarely vote against each other.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you play without a narrator?

Yes, that's the whole point: the app is the narrator. It hands out the roles, wakes each role in turn during the night, announces the dead at dawn and counts the votes. Nobody has to sit out to run the table.

### Can you play remotely?

Yes, and only online: everyone sees their role on their phone, the wolves have their private chat, the village debates on voice chat or in the text chat. Share a code or a QR, and the table is full.

### Can you play without alcohol?

Yes. Werewolf is part of the site's alcohol-free mode: the sips disappear, the game stays the same.

### Can you play alone, with bots?

Yes. "Try it with bots" starts a private table with three bots — the four-player minimum — that vote, accuse and defend themselves. Your friends can join along the way.

### How long does a game last?

A night lasts 2 to 3 minutes (less once the roles have acted), the debate 3 minutes by default (the host sets it from 1 to 5), the vote 1 minute. Count on 15 to 30 minutes for a table of 6 to 8 players.

### Do you need an account?

No: a nickname is enough, the guest account is created on the spot, no email needed. You can save it later to keep your stats.
